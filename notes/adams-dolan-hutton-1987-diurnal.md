# Adams, Dolan, Hutton 1987 — diurnal lumbar mechanics

This paper contains **two different experiments**. Keep them separate during retrieval.

## Human experiment

Twenty-one volunteers had lumbar flexion measured with electronic inclinometers early in the morning and in the afternoon.

Reported result: measured lumbar flexion range increased by about **5°** during the day.

This directly supports a within-day difference in measured flexion range under this protocol. It does not directly measure disc stress, ligament stress, muscle stiffness, extension range, or pain.

## Cadaver experiment

Cadaveric lumbar motion segments were creep-loaded to represent a day's loading history, then their bending behavior was measured.

Reported result: creep loading reduced resistance to bending, with a particularly marked effect in the disc, and increased lumbar flexion range by **12.5°** in the experimental preparation.

This is direct evidence about the tested cadaveric segments. It is not a 12.5° expected change in a living person's daily flexion range.

## Combined inference in the paper

The authors combined the human and cadaver findings in a mechanical analysis and estimated substantially greater early-morning bending stress during forward bending, reporting increases of about **300% for discs** and **80% for neural-arch ligaments** relative to later-day conditions in their analysis.

Treat these percentages as **model-derived inferences combining the two experiments**, not stresses directly measured inside living volunteers.

## Useful retrieval claims

Supported:

- lumbar mechanical behavior is history-dependent;
- measured human flexion range can differ between early morning and afternoon;
- creep loading can reduce bending resistance in cadaveric lumbar motion segments;
- time of day can therefore be a proxy for preceding recumbency/loading history in some lumbar-flexion questions.

Not supported by this paper alone:

- the entire back is globally `tighter` in the morning;
- extension behaves the same way as flexion;
- a particular person's morning pain is caused by disc stress;
- a mild supported supine stretch has the same loading as forward bending;
- morning stretching is categorically good or bad.
