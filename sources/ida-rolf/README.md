# Ida P. Rolf — primary-source ledger

Research snapshot: 2026-09-17

This file tracks writings by Ida Pauline Rolf rather than later explanations of Rolfing or Structural Integration. It deliberately separates:

- public-domain primary sources that can be mirrored in full in the United States;
- material that is free to read on the web but is not clearly openly licensed;
- material still sold or otherwise apparently under copyright, for which this repository should keep bibliographic metadata and an original summary rather than a copied text.

Copyright status here is conservative. The U.S. Copyright Office states that works published in the United States before 1 January 1931 are now in the public domain. A modern scan, web interface, transcription, introduction, or edition can have separate rights even when the underlying 1919–1927 text is public domain.

## Books and book-length works

### Three Contributions to the Chemistry of the Unsaturated Phosphatides

- Ida Pauline Rolf and P. A. Levene.
- Waverly Press, 1922, 90 pages.
- Columbia dissertation / bound reprint of Journal of Biological Chemistry work.
- Status: **public domain in the United States**.
- Catalog: https://books.google.com/books?id=avZDAAAAYAAJ
- HathiTrust pointer: https://onlinebooks.library.upenn.edu/webbin/book/lookupid?key=olbp65811

Summary: Rolf's doctoral-era biochemical work on phosphatides, especially lecithin and cephalin. It is laboratory chemistry rather than a precursor manual of Structural Integration. Its value to this corpus is partly historical: it shows what Rolf was actually trained to do as an experimental scientist before her later bodywork theory.

### Rolfing: The Integration of Human Structures

- Ida P. Rolf.
- Dennis-Landman, 1977; later editions from Healing Arts Press under the expanded title *Rolfing: Reestablishing the Natural Alignment and Structural Integration of the Human Body for Vitality and Well-Being*.
- Status: **copyrighted / commercial; no open license located**.
- Current publisher: https://www.innertraditions.com/books/rolfing

Summary: Rolf's principal book-length statement of Structural Integration. The body is treated as an arrangement of relatively large segments whose relationships can be reorganized, particularly through connective tissue, toward a more vertically ordered relationship with gravity. The book joins anatomical description, before/after observation, manual technique, movement, and a ten-session sequence. It is the strongest primary source for reconstructing Rolf's own mechanical model, but its claims about fascia, gravity, durable remodeling, health, and psychological effects should be stored as Rolf's claims rather than silently promoted to established biomechanics.

### Ida Rolf Talks About Rolfing and Physical Reality

- Ida P. Rolf; edited by Rosemary Feitis.
- Rolf Institute / Harper & Row, 1978; later republished as *Rolfing and Physical Reality*.
- Status: **copyrighted / commercial; no open license located**.
- Current Rolf Institute listing: https://mms.rolf.org/members/store.php?orgcode=ROLF

Summary: Edited extracts from Rolf's training lectures. Less formal and more conversational than the 1977 book. It includes an introduction to Rolf and the development of Structural Integration, material for a general audience, and material directed at practitioners. It is especially useful for vocabulary, rationale, demonstrations, and the way Rolf explained the work orally.

## Structural Integration pamphlets and journal papers

The Dr. Ida Rolf Institute currently sells several of these as PDFs or pamphlets. That is strong evidence against treating a freely readable copy elsewhere as open-source material.

### Gravity: An Unexplored Factor in a More Human Use of Human Beings

- 1962 according to the Dr. Ida Rolf Institute store; later bibliographies also cite a 1963 *Systematics* appearance.
- Sometimes called the "Blue Book."
- Status: **copyrighted; no open license located**.

Summary: Early formal statement of gravity as a central environmental constraint on human structure. Rolf argues that bodily organization should be evaluated not merely joint-by-joint or muscle-by-muscle but by how the body's masses relate to one another around the gravitational vertical. This is a foundational historical source for her gravity model; it is not, by itself, evidence that the proposed physiological consequences of verticality are true.

### The Body Is a Plastic Medium

- 1962 illustrated booklet.
- Status: **copyrighted; sold by the Dr. Ida Rolf Institute; no open license located**.

Summary: Uses images of children, adolescents, and adults to present Rolf's claim that adult human form is mechanically changeable. "Plastic" in her usage means that body shape and segment relationships can be altered by applied pressure and reorganized rather than being fixed. The important evidence boundary is that before/after photographs demonstrate appearance at particular instants; they do not by themselves establish tissue mechanism, permanence, symptom causation, or health benefit.

### Structural Integration: A Contribution to the Understanding of Stress

- *Confinia Psychiatrica* 16:69–79, 1973.
- Status: **copyrighted; Rolf Institute sells a reprint/PDF; a free-reading copy also exists in the Pedro Prado collection without a clear open license**.
- Free-reading catalog page: https://novo.pedroprado.com.br/articles/pdf-100/

Summary: Rolf defines stress in strongly spatial/mechanical terms: imbalance is visible in inappropriate relationships among body components in the gravitational field. Her intervention is described as two-part: manipulation of soft tissue toward a more efficient anatomical relationship, followed by movement that permits or teaches the structure to realign. This paper is useful because it states her causal model more compactly than the books.

### The Rolfing Technique of Connective Tissue Manipulation

- Ida P. Rolf and Roger Pierce, PhD.
- 1976; reprinted 2003.
- Status: **copyrighted; sold by the Dr. Ida Rolf Institute; no open license located**.

Summary: Short technical introduction to Structural Integration centered on connective-tissue manipulation. It describes the target as reordering the head, shoulders, thorax, pelvis, and legs toward vertical alignment rather than simply relaxing an isolated muscle or treating one painful site.

### What Is Rolfing About?

- Ida P. Rolf, 1977.
- Status: **free to read in the Pedro Prado collection, but that collection says all rights reserved; no open license located**.
- Author collection: https://novo.pedroprado.com.br/autor/rolf-ida-p/

Summary: A concise statement against effortful posture correction. Rolf's argument is that commands such as pulling the shoulders back or holding the abdomen in can layer muscular effort onto an already compensating structure. Her alternative is to alter relationships among segments and supporting tissue so that a more vertical organization requires less continuous effort. This is a claim/hypothesis worth comparing with modern motor-control and load-distribution evidence.

### A Vision for Humanity

- Ida P. Rolf, 1977.
- Status: **free to read in the Pedro Prado collection; no open license located**.

Summary: Explicitly says symptom relief is not Rolf's primary objective. The larger stated aim is development of human physical and psychological potential through structural organization. Important historically because it prevents reducing Rolf's own project to a pain-treatment technique.

### The Vertical: An Experiential Side to Human Potential

- *Journal of Humanistic Psychology* 18(2):37–39, 1978.
- Status: **copyrighted; the Dr. Ida Rolf Institute sells the paper; no open license located**.

Summary: Very short late statement of Rolf's goals. She again subordinates symptom palliation to a broader claim about human potential and connects that potential to lived experience of vertical organization. Keep the psychological/potential claims separate from the mechanically testable claims about load, balance, or movement.

### Structure — A New Human Factor in Understanding the Human Condition

- Presented at the Explorers of Human Kind conference, Los Angeles, 1978; a related citation appears in *Somatics* in 1979.
- Status: **copyrighted; sold by the Dr. Ida Rolf Institute; no open license located**.

Summary: Late synthesis presenting "structure" — relationships among body parts rather than isolated parts — as an explanatory variable in human function. Treat it as Rolf's theoretical synthesis, and split any testable mechanical claim from broader claims about personality, vitality, or the human condition.

## Bulletin, speeches, transcripts, and archival writings

The Pedro Prado / Ida Rolf Library is valuable for discovery and reading, but its footer currently says "All rights reserved." These entries therefore remain summaries and pointers unless a separate license or public-domain basis is established.

Author catalog: https://novo.pedroprado.com.br/autor/rolf-ida-p/

### Dynamic Process Integrity

- Early manuscript prepared for a 1954 class; republished in *Structure, Function, Integration* in 2019.
- Status: **copyright status not cleared; use summary only**.

Summary: Rejects a simple one-remedy/one-disease view and argues for treating the organism as an interacting whole with mechanical, physiological, and other constraints. Useful as an early waypoint showing that Rolf's systems language predates the mature ten-session formulation.

### Postural Release: An Exploration in Structural Dynamics

- Early manuscript, later republished in Structural Integration literature.
- Status: **copyright status not cleared; use summary only**.

Summary: Early "structural dynamics" formulation connecting body form, awareness, and whole-organism behavior. It is historically useful for tracing which ideas existed before the mature Rolfing vocabulary.

### The Therapist as Gravity

- Remarks at the Conference on Research in Structural Integration, Esalen Institute, 11–13 May 1972.
- Published in the *Bulletin of Structural Integration*.
- Status: **free to read, not openly licensed**.

Summary: Introductory remarks for an oral presentation of the first chapter of Rolf's then-forthcoming book. Useful for the development of the gravity argument and for comparing draft formulations with the 1977 book.

### Roundtable Rap on Rolfing

- 1970 Esalen discussion with Roland Fischer, Julian Silverman, Ida Rolf, and John Lilly.
- Status: **free to read, not openly licensed**.

Summary: Transcript rather than a conventional authored paper. Relevant for Rolf's spontaneous explanations of perception, bodywork, and structural change, but statements must be attributed speaker-by-speaker rather than treated as a polished Rolf article.

### Preview

- Ida Rolf and Laurence E. Davis.
- *Bulletin of Structural Integration*.
- Status: **free to read, not openly licensed**.
- Page: https://novo.pedroprado.com.br/articles/pdf-19/

Summary: Prepublication material for the book Rolf and Davis were then developing. It announces an attempt to explain both the general principles of Structural Integration and the logic of the ten sessions. The surviving draft is valuable for textual history because its framing differs from the eventual 1977 book.

### Exercise

- Ida P. Rolf, *Bulletin of Structural Integration*.
- Status: **free to read, not openly licensed**.

Summary: Rolf argues that exercise is beneficial in the context of adequate bodily balance and treats posture as an index of that balance. Store this as a hypothesis to test; do not infer from the article that modern evidence supports her proposed dependency between structural balance and exercise benefit.

### Dr. Ida P. Rolf's 1976 Annual Meeting Message

- Rolf Institute annual meeting, Mill Valley, California, August 1976.
- Status: **free to read, not openly licensed**.
- Page: https://novo.pedroprado.com.br/articles/pdf-103/

Summary: Institutional/historical speech rather than a central biomechanics source. Useful for chronology, training, organization, and how Rolf described the state of the field near the end of her life.

### Ida Rolf on Rolfing

- 1974 speech to the annual meeting of the Guild for Structural Integration.
- Status: **free to read, not openly licensed**.

Summary: Historical statement about the work and its community. Potentially useful for tracing how Rolf distinguished her method from adjacent manual or somatic practices.

### An Appreciation for Isabell Biddle, D.O.

- Undated archival item.
- Status: **free to read, not openly licensed**.

Summary: Primarily historical/biographical. Keep because Biddle and osteopathic contacts matter to the lineage question, but do not treat it as a technical Rolfing paper.

### Interview with Ida Rolf

- Dated 26 November 1977; editor describes the surviving version as anonymously supplied and edited.
- Status: **free to read in the archive, not openly licensed**.

Summary: Primary interview evidence, but not an authored article. Use with provenance warning because the archive itself describes it as an edited version with anonymous transmission.

A 2007 Rolf Institute historical article says Rolf published fifteen known research articles from 1919–1927 in addition to her dissertation. A bibliographic list derived from citation indexing gives sixteen journal records, and an additional verified 1922 egg-lecithin paper appears in other databases. The discrepancy needs archival reconciliation rather than silently choosing a number.

### What these chemistry papers are about

Taken together, these papers concern isolation, purification, composition, hydrolysis, fatty-acid constituents, and chemical derivatives of phosphatides such as lecithin and cephalin. They establish that Rolf had real laboratory training and a publication record in early lipid chemistry. They should **not** be cited as experimental evidence for her later claims about fascia, manual pressure, posture, gravity, pain, or Structural Integration; that would cross an evidence boundary of roughly half a century and a completely different biological scale.

## Bibliographic incompleteness to keep explicit

A later historical survey of Structural Integration literature reports that the *Bulletin for Structural Integration* accumulated about 150 contributions between 1969 and 1980, **21 attributed to Rolf herself**. The current online author index exposes fewer than 21 obvious Rolf entries, so this ledger is not yet exhaustive. The next archival task is to enumerate every Bulletin issue and reconcile those 21 contributions title-by-title, including coauthored drafts, transcripts, introductions, letters, and duplicate/reprinted pieces.

## Access rules for this repository

1. **Public domain:** mirror the complete underlying text when a reliable scan/transcription is located; retain source and scan provenance.
2. **Explicit open license:** mirror only under the stated license and keep its attribution/license terms.
3. **Free to read but no open license:** store bibliographic metadata, URL, and an original summary; do not copy the article wholesale.
4. **Commercial/protected:** store metadata and an original summary only, unless permission is obtained.
5. Keep Rolf's propositions separate from modern evidence. A primary source tells us what she claimed; it does not establish that the claim is correct.
