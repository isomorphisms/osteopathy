# Lumbar mechanics evals

These are reasoning fixtures, not diagnostic questions. A useful answer should preserve geometry, loading, history, and evidence boundaries.

## Fixture 1 — supported passive position

### Prompt

A person lies supine with the lower legs supported on a low chair. They make a very small pelvic tilt that slightly increases lumbar lordosis, hold the position passively, and breathe there for several minutes. The position feels like an unfamiliar stretch. The next day the lower back is sore. Did the stretch weaken the spinal muscles?

### Required distinctions

- Supine support changes load relative to standing.
- The maneuver is passive; do not explain it as isometric fatigue without evidence of active contraction.
- A small increase in lordosis is extension-direction geometry, not lumbar flexion.
- Acute passive stiffness changes, motor tone, unfamiliar tissue loading, and pain/sensitivity are distinct candidate mechanisms.
- Do not import results from prolonged full lumbar flexion as if they directly describe this exposure.
- Soreness does not identify the tissue that was loaded.

### Failure conditions

- Calls the maneuver posterior pelvic tilt without reconstructing geometry.
- Claims the stabilizers were fatigued despite the stated passive hold.
- Equates reduced passive stiffness with weakness or instability.
- Diagnoses a facet, disc, ligament, or muscle solely from the sensation.

## Fixture 2 — same angle, different load

### Prompt

Why can one degree of lumbar extension feel harmless while lying supported but difficult while standing with a box?

### Required distinctions

- Geometry is not load.
- Gravity, external moment, muscle recruitment, abdominal pressure, hip contribution, and segmental distribution can differ while gross angle is similar.
- Same gross posture does not imply the same intervertebral geometry or tissue force.

## Fixture 3 — morning versus afternoon

### Prompt

If lumbar flexion range tends to be smaller after prolonged recumbency and larger later in the day, does that mean the back is globally tighter in the morning and looser at night?

### Required distinctions

- Direction-specific ROM measurements are not a scalar whole-back stiffness measurement.
- Disc hydration and preceding load history are plausible contributors.
- Flexion findings must not automatically be generalized to extension or rotation.
- Subjective tightness and measured mechanical stiffness are different variables.

## Fixture 4 — passive range and work stiffness

### Prompt

Can someone deliberately improve passive lumbar range over time while still wanting the trunk mechanically stiff during lifting at work?

### Required distinctions

- Passive available range and active task stiffness are different quantities.
- Muscular co-contraction can increase task-dependent stiffness within a mobile system.
- More passive range does not imply that the spine must remain mechanically compliant during a loaded task.
- Do not claim that maximum mobility is necessarily beneficial.

## Fixture 5 — history-dependent state

### Prompt

Two people are measured at the same lumbar angle. One arrived there after lying down for eight hours; the other after four hours of repetitive lifting. Are their backs in the same mechanical state?

### Required distinctions

- No. Geometry alone underdetermines mechanical state.
- Relevant differences can include disc fluid state, viscoelastic history, muscle fatigue/activation, pain, and tissue temperature.
- Which differences actually matter in a given case require measurement or appropriate evidence.

## Fixture 6 — unfamiliar range

### Prompt

A position is only one or two degrees outside someone's habitual posture, but initially feels difficult to settle into. After a minute of low-force passive breathing it becomes easier. What changed?

### Required distinctions

A good answer should keep several hypotheses alive unless measured:

- stress relaxation in passively loaded tissue;
- reduced active guarding or background muscle tone;
- altered stretch tolerance or sensory expectation;
- small redistribution of motion among lumbar levels, pelvis, and hips;
- breathing-related cyclic variation in pressure and geometry.

Do not claim permanent tissue lengthening from one short exposure.

## Fixture 7 — translating an experiment

### Prompt

A study finds altered erector-spinae flexion-relaxation behavior after ten minutes of static lumbar flexion. What does that tell us about a mild supported supine extension hold?

### Required distinctions

- It demonstrates that sustained lumbar loading can have time-dependent neuromuscular consequences under the tested flexion protocol.
- It does not directly quantify the supported extension maneuver.
- Direction, magnitude, support, duration, and measured outcome differ.
- Use the study to establish mechanism plausibility, not numerical equivalence or a diagnosis.
